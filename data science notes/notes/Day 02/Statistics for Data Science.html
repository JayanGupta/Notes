Statistics & Probability

The foundational math required to find signals inside raw data noise.

Measures of Central Tendency

Before modeling data, you must locate its "center". There are three main ways to do this:

  • Mean: The arithmetic average of all values.
  • Median: The exact middle value when ordered.
  • Mode: The value that appears most frequently.

Each metric tells a unique story depending on whether your dataset has severe distortions.

Why not just use the average?

Imagine a room with 4 workers earning $40k/yr. The average is $40k. Suddenly, a billionaire walking into the room shifts the average to $200 Million, completely misrepresenting the real group dynamics!

Data Science Insight

We check central values during initial EDA to notice if data distribution leans aggressively to one side (Skewness).

Mean vs. Median

Let's look at the mathematical formula for the population Mean (μ):

Population Mean Formula: μ = ( Σ Xᵢ ) / N

The Outlier Effect: The Mean takes every single value into account, making it highly sensitive to extreme values.

The Median splits the data in half, ignoring the size of extreme edges.

Understanding the Mode

The Mode represents the highest density of frequency. Unlike Mean and Median, the Mode can be applied to categorical non-numerical data (e.g., most popular car color).

  • Unimodal: One clear peak.
  • Bimodal: Two distinct frequent peaks (often points to two mixed sub-populations).

Measures of Dispersion

Knowing the center isn't enough. Two datasets can have the exact same mean but look completely different.

Variance (σ²): Measures the average squared distance from the mean.

Population Variance Formula: σ² = Σ (Xᵢ - μ)² / N

Because we square the distances, the units become squared (e.g., if data is dollars, variance is dollars squared), which is hard to interpret.

Dataset A: [10, 10, 10, 10]

Mean = 10 | Variance = 0 (No spread)

Dataset B: [0, 5, 15, 20]

Mean = 10 | Variance = 62.5 (High spread)

Standard Deviation (σ)

To fix the squared unit problem, we take the square root of the variance. This brings us back to the original unit scale.

Standard Deviation Formula: σ = √[ Σ (Xᵢ - μ)² / N ]

A low σ means points are closely bunched around the mean (highly predictable).

A high σ means wide variability and volatility (risky/unpredictable).

Percentiles & Quartiles

Quartiles divide your ordered data into 4 equal quarters:

  • Q1 (25th Percentile): Splits bottom 25% of data.
  • Q2 (50th Percentile): The Median.
  • Q3 (75th Percentile): Splits top 25% of data.

The Interquartile Range (IQR) = Q3 - Q1. It captures the middle 50% of your data points, stripping away outliers.

In Data Science, anything outside 1.5 × IQR below Q1 or above Q3 is mathematically classified as an outlier!

Correlation vs. Causation

Correlation (r): Measures the linear strength between two variables. Ranges from -1 to +1.

  • +1: Perfect positive movement.
  • -1: Perfect opposite movement.
  • 0: Absolute chaos / random noise.

The Cardinal Rule of Data Science:

Just because variable X tracks perfectly with variable Y does not mean X causes Y! They might both be driven by a hidden third variable.

Example: Ice Cream Sales & Shark Attacks both rise in summer because of warm weather, not because ice cream triggers sharks!

Introduction to Probability

Probability quantifies how likely an event is to occur. It ranges from 0 (impossible) to 1 (certain).

Basic Probability Formula: P(A) = |Favorable Outcomes| / |Sample Space|
  • Sample Space (S): All possible outcomes.
  • Event (A): A specific outcome subset.
  • Complement (A'): P(A') = 1 − P(A).

Addition Rule of Probability

When events can't happen together (mutually exclusive):

Mutually Exclusive Events: P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B)

When events can overlap (non-mutually exclusive):

General Addition Rule: P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∩ B)

Example: Drawing a card that is a King OR a Heart from a deck. Kings and Hearts overlap at the King of Hearts!

Conditional Probability

The probability of event A occurring given that B already happened. Knowing B shrinks your universe.

Conditional Probability Formula: P(A | B) = P(A ∩ B) / P(B)

Real-World Example: If a medical test is 99% accurate, and you test positive, what's the probability you actually have the disease?

Bayes' Theorem

Bayes' Theorem lets us update beliefs when presented with new evidence. It flips conditional probability around.

Bayes' Theorem: P(A|B) = [ P(B|A) × P(A) ] / P(B)
  • P(A): Prior — initial belief before evidence.
  • P(B|A): Likelihood — how likely is the evidence if A is true.
  • P(B): Evidence — total probability of observing this evidence.
  • P(A|B): Posterior — updated belief after evidence.

Example: An email contains the word "FREE". Is it spam? Adjust the sliders to tune the Bayesian classifier!

Common Probability Distributions

📊 Bernoulli / Binomial

Binary outcomes: Success or Failure.

Binomial PMF: P(X=k) = C(n,k) pᵏ (1−p)ⁿ⁻ᵏ
  • Coin flips, pass/fail, click/no-click
  • Fixed number of independent trials
  • DS Use: A/B testing, logistic regression foundations

⚡ Poisson

Count of rare events in a fixed interval.

Poisson PMF: P(X=k) = (λᵏ e⁻λ) / k!
  • Customer arrivals, server errors/hr
  • Events are independent, rate (λ) is constant
  • DS Use: Anomaly detection, queue optimization

🔔 Normal (Gaussian)

The bell curve — king of distributions.

68-95-99.7 Rule: 68% within ±1σ, 95% within ±2σ
  • Heights, test scores, measurement errors
  • Central Limit Theorem guarantees its prevalence
  • DS Use: Z-scores, hypothesis testing, feature scaling

Data Science Statistics & Probability Cheat Sheet

Use this reference framework when profiling a new dataset during EDA:

Statistical Metric What it measures When to trust it Data Science Application
Mean Arithmetic Center When distribution is symmetrical & clean Baseline prediction, feature scaling
Median Positional Middle When the data contains heavy outliers Imputing missing values in skewed columns
Mode Highest Frequency Categorical or multi-modal states Filling missing string classifications
Standard Dev. Data Dispersion / Spread Always useful alongside the mean Detecting anomaly thresholds & Z-scores
IQR Middle 50% Range Robust to extreme noise edge limits Automated cleaning of rogue outliers
Probability Likelihood of events When sample space is well-defined Feature likelihood estimation
Cond. Probability Event likelihood given evidence When events are dependent Naive Bayes classifiers, filtering
Bayes' Theorem Updated belief with new evidence When prior knowledge exists Spam filters, medical diagnosis, ML priors
